# 1. Market Background

## Seoul as a Global Business City

Seoul has become one of Asia’s major business hubs.

Especially the three major business districts:

- GBD (Gangnam Business District)
- YBD (Yeouido Business District)
- CBD (Central Business District)

continue to attract:

- Global corporations
- Technology companies
- Financial institutions
- Startups
- Professional services firms
- International talent

As companies become more global, employee mobility is increasing.

However, Korea’s residential market has not yet developed a standardized Corporate Housing infrastructure.

---

# 2. Core Problem

## Housing Exists.
## Corporate Housing Does Not.

Seoul has a large amount of housing inventory:

- Apartments
- Officetels
- Villas
- Studios

However, most housing supply was designed for individual tenants, not corporate users.

The problem is not a lack of housing.

The problem is:

> Existing housing is not structured, standardized, or operated for corporate living.

# 5. Why Existing Players Have Not Solved This

## Korea’s Unique Market Structure

Corporate Housing has developed slowly because of:

### 1. Jeonse Culture

Historically, Korea focused on:
- Ownership
- Long-term leases
- Large deposits

rather than flexible monthly living.

---

### 2. Fragmented Ownership

Unlike institutional rental markets overseas,
many Korean residential assets are owned individually.

This makes standardization difficult.

---

### 3. Complex Regulations

Housing operation requires understanding:

- Residential leases
- Corporate contracts
- Foreign resident registration
- Utility management
- Tenant protection regulations

Operational complexity creates barriers to entry.
